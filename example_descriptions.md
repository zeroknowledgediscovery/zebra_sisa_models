# MOCK CLINICAL HISTORY 1 — RECURRENT DEPRESSION WITH ANTIPSYCHOTIC TREATMENT

The patient is a 50-year-old male with a history of recurrent major depressive disorder dating back to approximately 2001. He initially presented with persistent sadness, fatigue, insomnia, and difficulty maintaining employment. He was treated intermittently with fluoxetine and later switched to venlafaxine in 2009 following worsening symptoms. In 2014, quetiapine was added for persistent depressive symptoms, irritability, and sleep disturbances. He experienced another significant depressive episode in 2019, characterized by social withdrawal, poor appetite, and reduced daily functioning. During a routine visit in 2023, he reported feelings of hopelessness and difficulty coping with family stressors. He continues to receive psychiatric medications, although adherence has been inconsistent. No suicide attempts or explicit suicidal ideation have been documented.


# MOCK CLINICAL HISTORY 2 — DEPRESSION WITH AMBIGUOUS INJURY HISTORY

The patient is a 50-year-old man with longstanding depression and anxiety, first diagnosed around 2004 following prolonged occupational stress and marital difficulties. Initial treatment included sertraline, with aripiprazole added in 2011 for inadequate response to antidepressant monotherapy. In 2016, he presented to an emergency department following superficial lacerations to his left forearm, reportedly sustained during an incident at home. The circumstances were not fully clarified, and no formal documentation established intentional self-injury. Subsequent psychiatric evaluations in 2018 and 2021 noted persistent depressive symptoms, irritability, sleep disruption, and occasional expressions of worthlessness. In 2024, he underwent another medication adjustment after reporting increased social isolation and reduced motivation. There is no confirmed history of suicidal behavior or documented suicidal intent.


# MOCK CLINICAL HISTORY 3 — CHRONIC DEPRESSION WITH FUNCTIONAL DECLINE

The patient is a 50-year-old male with a documented psychiatric history beginning in approximately 1999, when he sought treatment for depressed mood, anxiety, and recurrent insomnia. He was initially prescribed paroxetine and subsequently transitioned to duloxetine in 2010 because of persistent symptoms. In 2013, low-dose risperidone was prescribed during a period of marked agitation and emotional instability, although no formal psychotic disorder was diagnosed. Between 2017 and 2020, he had multiple primary care and psychiatric visits for worsening depression, chronic fatigue, and difficulty managing interpersonal relationships. A 2022 emergency department encounter documented an episode of excessive alcohol consumption and an unexplained minor hand injury, without evidence establishing deliberate self-harm. By 2025, he continued to describe diminished interest in previously enjoyable activities, feelings of isolation, and intermittent hopelessness. No explicit suicidal ideation, plan, or prior suicide attempt was recorded in the available clinical history.
